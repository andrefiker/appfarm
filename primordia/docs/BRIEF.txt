==================================================
CORE IDEA
==================================================

PRIMORDIA is an original evolution sandbox inspired by the broad fantasy of games such as Spore’s microorganism stage:

START AS A TINY ORGANISM
→ SWIM
→ EAT
→ SURVIVE
→ GROW
→ MUTATE
→ REPRODUCE
→ ADAPT
→ COMPETE
→ EVOLVE INTO SOMETHING DISTINCT

Do NOT copy Spore’s:

- creatures
- art
- sounds
- user interface
- editor layout
- names
- assets
- exact progression
- levels
- animations
- visual identity

Create an original biological world and game system.

The ambition is:

“what I remember Spore’s cell stage feeling like, but deeper, more systemic, stranger, and built as a modern small indie game.”

==================================================
PLATFORM
==================================================

PRIMARY TARGET:
Windows 10/11 desktop PC.

This game should work well on a modest PC.

Prefer a lightweight engine and architecture.

Godot 4 is preferred if practical.

Do NOT require:

- cloud backend
- login
- online account
- analytics
- external server
- internet connection

Everything should run locally and offline.

==================================================
PLAYER EXPERIENCE
==================================================

The game begins almost immediately.

Minimal title screen:

PRIMORDIA

NEW ORGANISM
CONTINUE
SETTINGS
QUIT

Starting a new game should place the player into the primordial environment within seconds.

The first organism is tiny, weak, simple, and visually unimpressive.

Evolution must make it gradually feel like YOUR creature.

The player should eventually look at the organism and think:

“How the hell did I end up becoming this thing?”

==================================================
WORLD
==================================================

Create a large continuous microscopic ecosystem.

Visual inspiration:

- pond water
- plankton
- microscope photography
- translucent tissue
- suspended particles
- cellular structures
- bioluminescent organisms
- deep-water microbial ecosystems

But use a stylized original visual language rather than photorealism.

The environment should feel alive at all times.

Include:

- drifting organic particles
- nutrient clouds
- currents
- light gradients
- debris
- microbial colonies
- predators
- prey
- scavengers
- parasites
- neutral organisms

Organisms should exist and interact even when the player ignores them.

==================================================
MOVEMENT
==================================================

Movement should feel biological rather than like a spaceship.

Support:

WASD:
directional movement

Mouse:
aim / orient organism

SPACE:
burst action or primary ability

SHIFT:
secondary ability where appropriate

Movement characteristics should be affected by morphology.

Examples:

Flagella:
strong forward propulsion

Cilia:
high maneuverability

Broad fins/membranes:
efficient turning

Large organisms:
more inertia

Streamlined organisms:
higher top speed

Heavy armor:
slower acceleration

Movement should visually correspond to anatomy.

==================================================
ENERGY SYSTEM
==================================================

All biological actions consume energy.

Energy comes from:

- consuming organisms
- photosynthesis
- scavenging
- nutrient absorption
- symbiosis
- specialized later mutations

Energy powers:

- movement
- attacks
- defensive abilities
- healing
- reproduction
- growth

Avoid dozens of resource bars.

Keep the core readable.

==================================================
DIETS
==================================================

Allow organisms to evolve toward multiple strategies.

Examples:

HERBIVORE / FILTER FEEDER
absorbs nutrients and microorganisms

PREDATOR
actively hunts other creatures

SCAVENGER
feeds on dead organisms

PHOTOSYNTHETIC
gains energy from illuminated regions

PARASITIC
attaches to larger organisms

OMNIVORE
flexible but less specialized

SYMBIOTIC
benefits from relationships with another organism type

Do not hard-lock the player into a class.

Behavior should emerge from anatomy and mutations.

==================================================
CREATURE BODY SYSTEM
==================================================

The creature must be physically assembled from functional biological parts.

Core body:

- membrane/body mass
- nucleus/core
- mouth structures
- sensory structures
- propulsion structures
- defensive structures
- offensive structures
- metabolic structures

Parts should have meaningful tradeoffs.

Examples:

FLAGELLUM
+ speed
+ acceleration
- energy consumption

CILIA
+ turning
+ fine movement
- less efficient at high speed

SPIKE
+ collision damage
- increased drag

ARMOR PLATE
+ damage resistance
- mass
- turning penalty

TOXIN GLAND
+ poison attack
- substantial energy cost

PHOTORECEPTOR
+ better awareness / visibility
- mutation cost

CHEMORECEPTOR
+ detect food through obscured areas

FILTER MOUTH
+ efficient passive feeding
- poor against large prey

PREDATORY JAW
+ high damage
- costly
- requires proximity

PHOTOSYNTHETIC ORGANELLE
+ passive energy in light
- reduced efficiency in darkness

FAT STORAGE
+ energy reserves
- mass

REGENERATION TISSUE
+ healing
- high metabolic cost

==================================================
EVOLUTION EDITOR
==================================================

Create an excellent organism editor.

The player enters it when reproducing or reaching a mutation threshold.

The organism should appear enlarged.

The player can modify its body using earned mutation points.

Allow:

- adding parts
- removing parts
- moving parts
- rotating parts
- resizing within sensible limits
- changing body proportions
- adjusting symmetry
- changing coloration/patterns

The editor must immediately communicate stat changes.

Show only important effects:

Speed
Turn
Defense
Attack
Energy
Senses

Avoid spreadsheet overload.

Creature shape should affect actual gameplay.

Do not make morphology purely cosmetic.

==================================================
GENETICS / MUTATION
==================================================

Evolution should involve both deliberate and emergent variation.

At reproduction:

player receives mutation opportunities.

Offer several mutation choices based partly on:

- player behavior
- environment
- diet
- threats encountered
- anatomy
- ancestry

Examples:

Player escapes predators frequently:
movement-related adaptations become more likely.

Player hunts heavily:
predatory mutations become more likely.

Player lives near bright surface water:
photosynthetic adaptations become more likely.

Player receives repeated physical damage:
defensive adaptations become more likely.

This should create a feeling that the environment influences evolution.

==================================================
NPC EVOLUTION
==================================================

This is critically important.

Other organisms should ALSO evolve.

Do not spawn static enemies forever.

Track simplified inheritable characteristics for NPC species.

Allow population-level changes over generations.

Examples:

If player heavily hunts slow prey:
surviving prey gradually become faster.

If armored prey dominates:
predators may gradually gain stronger attacks.

If a predator becomes too successful:
its prey population falls and predator numbers may subsequently collapse.

If nutrient abundance changes:
different feeding strategies become viable.

This does NOT need a university-grade evolutionary simulation.

It DOES need visible ecosystem adaptation.

==================================================
SPECIES SYSTEM
==================================================

Procedurally generate species based on modular anatomy.

Each species should possess:

- body shape
- color/pattern
- movement method
- diet
- aggression
- preferred depth
- senses
- defenses
- reproduction tendency

Species can:

- expand
- decline
- mutate
- split into related variants
- go extinct

Maintain a lightweight ecosystem ledger.

==================================================
FOOD WEB
==================================================

Create a functional food web.

Small organisms feed on nutrients.

Larger organisms may feed on smaller ones.

Dead organisms become scavenging opportunities.

Population pressure should matter.

Avoid infinite arbitrary spawning directly beside the player.

Spawn according to ecosystem and biome logic.

==================================================

==================================================

Create several connected ecological zones.

Examples:

SUNLIT SHALLOWS

high photosynthesis
many small organisms

ALGAL BLOOM

low visibility

OPEN WATER

TRANSITION ZONES

Do not use loading screens between ordinary zones if avoidable.

==================================================
SCALE
==================================================

As the organism evolves, slowly change perceived scale.

Early:
individual particles feel large.

Later:
tiny organisms become almost irrelevant.

Eventually:
the player competes with significantly larger creatures.

Do this through camera, world scaling, ecology, and creature growth.

Avoid endlessly making every NPC scale numerically with the player.

Small organisms should remain small.

==================================================
COMBAT
==================================================

Combat should be mostly physical/systemic.

Examples:

- biting
- piercing
- poisoning
- ramming
- grappling
- attaching
- stunning
- defensive discharge
- camouflage
- escape burst

Bodies should visually respond to impacts.

Avoid health-bar swordfight behavior.

Position and anatomy should matter.

==================================================
SENSORY SYSTEM
==================================================

Different organisms should perceive the world differently.

Player mutations can improve:

- visual radius
- chemical detection
- vibration detection
- threat awareness
- food detection

Visualize these elegantly.

Examples:

chemical trails
subtle pulses
directional indicators
faint outlines

Do not clutter the screen with giant HUD widgets.

==================================================
LIFE CYCLE
==================================================

Core loop:

EXPLORE
→ FEED
→ SURVIVE
→ BUILD ENERGY
→ REPRODUCE
→ MUTATE
→ RETURN TO WORLD

Death should matter but not be brutally punishing.

Maintain lineage progression.

Possible structure:

Death returns player to the latest viable descendant.

Some accumulated evolutionary progress survives through lineage genetics.

==================================================
LINEAGE
==================================================

Track the player's evolutionary lineage.

Show a simple branching timeline.

Each reproductive generation should save:

- generation number
- approximate morphology
- major mutations
- ecological role
- approximate size

Allow the player to see their organism changing over time.

This feature should feel delightful but remain lightweight.

==================================================
PROCEDURAL CREATURE VISUALS
==================================================

Creature bodies should appear soft, translucent, wet, cellular, and alive.

Use:

- layered translucent shapes
- procedural membranes
- organelles
- subtle deformation
- animated internal structures
- simple shaders where performant
- appendage animation
- body wobble
- reactive motion

Avoid looking like flat circles with eyes.

NO cartoon eyes.

NO smiling faces.

NO generic cute mobile-game creatures.

Aim for:

beautiful
alien
biological
slightly unsettling

==================================================
ANIMATION
==================================================

Organism movement should have secondary motion.

Examples:

- membranes flex
- tails trail
- cilia oscillate
- internal structures drift
- bodies compress on impact
- predators lunge
- organisms recoil when damaged

Even simple procedural animation is preferable to static sprites.

==================================================
SIMULATION PERFORMANCE
==================================================

Do NOT simulate every organism at maximum fidelity globally.

Use multiple simulation layers.

Near player:
full physical simulation.

Medium distance:
simplified behavioral simulation.

Far ecosystem:
statistical population simulation.

This should allow the world to feel much larger than the number of actively rendered creatures.

Save safely and atomically where practical.

DIFFICULTY

Examples:

- XP levels
- battle passes

- daily rewards

==================================================
GAME LENGTH
==================================================

The game should support a satisfying session in 20–40 minutes but allow a lineage to continue for hours.

There is no need for a traditional final boss.

A successful prototype/end-state can be reaching a sufficiently complex organism and stable ecological niche.
